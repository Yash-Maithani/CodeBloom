Programs handle a lot of text: names, messages, addresses, the lines of a file. A piece of text is called a **string**: a sequence of **characters**, such as letters, digits, spaces and punctuation. Like the items of a collection, the characters of a string have positions that start at 0 in C, C++, Java and Python.

Programs join strings, count their characters, search them and change their case. How a language stores text, and whether a string can be changed after it is made, differ a lot between languages, so each lesson explains its own language's rules.
