A decision with two outcomes, do this or do that, is often not enough. A ticket price can depend on three age groups, and a grade on four score ranges.

A program can handle this by checking conditions one after another, from the top. As soon as one condition is true, the program runs the instructions for that condition and skips all the others. If none is true, a final otherwise group runs, if there is one.

Because the conditions are checked in order, order matters: a condition that is true too often can take a value that was meant for a later condition. All four languages can do this. They differ in how they spell it.
